§ 01Your first 10 minutes4 steps

Your first 10 minutes

  1. 1

    Pick a free app

    You don’t need to buy anything. Photopea runs in your browser and opens almost every file here. On a phone, try PixelLab or Canva.

    See the free apps ↓
  2. 2

    Download a file

    Open any template or logo and press Download. If you see a ZIP, it holds every format in one file. Unzip it first.

  3. 3

    Open it and change it

    Open the file in your app. Swap the text, the colours and the pictures for your own. Every record page has a short How to use guide too.

  4. 4

    Export and share

    Save your finished design as a PNG or JPG to post online, or a PDF to print. Then show it off and tag Draft Studio.

§ 02File types, explainedWhat those letters mean

File types, explained

The letters after a file name tell you what kind of file it is and which apps can open it.

PSD

Photoshop file

A layered picture file. Most mockups are PSDs: you place your design into a special layer and it wraps onto the product.

Opens inPhotopea (free), Photoshop

AI

Illustrator file

The original, fully editable logo or vector design. Best when you want to change shapes, not just colours.

Opens inIllustrator, Photopea, Inkscape (free)

SVG

Vector graphic

Made of shapes, not pixels, so it stays sharp at any size. Perfect for logos and icons, on websites too.

Opens inFigma, Inkscape, Canva, Photopea (all free)

PDF

Print-ready document

Looks the same on every device. Send PDFs to print shops for business cards, flyers and posters.

Opens inAny phone or computer · edit in Illustrator or Inkscape

PNG

Picture with see-through background

A normal image that can have a transparent background. Use it to put a logo on top of photos, slides or videos.

Opens inEverything

JPG

Everyday photo

A small image file for sharing and posting. It can’t be see-through, so the background is always solid.

Opens inEverything

ZIP

A folder in one file

Holds several files together. Unzip it first: on a phone, tap it in your Files app; on a computer, double-click it.

Opens inFiles app (phone), Windows or Mac

§ 04How to use a mockupIn Photopea · free

How to use a mockup

A mockup shows your design on a real product, like a hoodie or a cup. Here’s how to do it for free in Photopea.

Browse mockups →
  1. Open the mockup. Go to photopea.com, press File → Open and choose the PSD you downloaded.
  2. Find the smart object. In the Layers panel, look for the layer with a small square badge on its picture, usually called something like “Your design here”.
  3. Double-click its picture. It opens in a new tab. This is where your design goes.
  4. Place your design. Press File → Open & Place, pick your logo or artwork, then drag the corners to size it.
  5. Save and close the tab. Press Ctrl + S (or Cmd + S on a Mac), then go back to the mockup tab. Your design is now on the product.
  6. Export it. Press File → Export as → JPG or PNG to get a picture you can post.
§ 05How to use a logoRename, recolour, export

How to use a logo

Every logo page has a Colour test, so you can see how a mark looks on light, dark and brand colours before you download it.

Browse logos →
  1. Open the vector file. Open the SVG in Figma or Inkscape, or the AI in Illustrator.
  2. Change the name. Click the wordmark and type your brand name. If the letters look different, the font isn’t on your device yet. Install it, or choose one you like.
  3. Recolour it. Select a shape and pick a new fill colour. Keep it to two or three colours.
  4. Export for screens. Save a PNG with a transparent background for social media, websites and videos.
  5. Export for print. Save a PDF or SVG for business cards, T-shirts and signs. It stays sharp at any size.
§ 06Words you’ll hearTap a word

Words you’ll hear

Designers use a lot of jargon. Here’s what the most common words actually mean.

Vector

A design made of shapes and lines instead of pixels. You can make it as big as a billboard and it stays sharp. SVG, AI and PDF files are usually vectors.

Raster (pixels)

A picture made of tiny coloured squares called pixels, like a photo. Zoom in too far and it goes blurry. PNG and JPG are raster files.

Layer

Designs are stacked like sheets of see-through paper. Each sheet is a layer, so you can move or change one part without touching the rest.

Smart object

A special Photoshop layer that holds another design inside it. Mockups use one so your artwork bends and shades like it’s really on the product.

Transparent background

No background at all: you can see through it. A logo with a transparent background sits cleanly on any photo or colour. Save as PNG to keep it.

Resolution

How many pixels an image has. More pixels means more detail. For print, aim for 300 pixels per inch; for screens, the size you need is enough.

RGB and CMYK

Two ways to make colour. RGB (red, green, blue light) is for screens. CMYK (four inks) is for printing. Ask your print shop which they need.

Mockup

A realistic picture of a product, like a hoodie, cup or sign, that you can put your own design on to show what it will look like.

Wordmark and logomark

A wordmark is a logo made of the brand name in a special style. A logomark is the symbol on its own. Many logos use both together.

Kerning

The space between two letters. Good kerning makes a word look even and easy to read, which matters most in logos and headlines.

Bleed

Extra design that runs past the edge of a printed page, so there’s no white strip when the printer trims it. Usually 3 mm on each side.

Font pairing

Choosing two fonts that look good together, often one for headings and one for body text. Two is usually enough; three at most.
